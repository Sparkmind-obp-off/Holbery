**Status:** ACTIVE EXECUTION

## 1. Purpose

This document converts the locked HOLBERY strategy into the next real-world action.

We are not trying to prove that HOLBERY can build software. That is already supported by engineering evidence.

We are trying to prove that HOLBERY can create something useful enough for a real person to exchange money for.

The first target is:

> **One real person → one legitimate purchase → one successful delivery → one honest feedback loop.**

No fake orders, no owner-funded test, no fabricated customer, no sandbox substitution.

## 2. Current Starting Point

### Proven

- HOLBERY direction is locked.
- Vision, mission and positioning are documented.
- Product governance and evidence framework exist.
- BRS-2026-V1 exists as a complete product artifact.
- Production commerce infrastructure is deployed and readiness-verified.
- Duitku production authentication and payment integration are technically verified.
- Private digital fulfillment is implemented and verified.

### Unproven

- Real market demand.
- Real customer willingness to pay.
- Real payment.
- Real customer outcome.
- Repeatability.
- Brand trust outside the builder/operator context.

Therefore the next move is **not another architecture cycle**.

It is customer-facing execution.

## 3. First Proof Target

### Product

**Barber Revenue Starter System — BRS-2026-V1**

### Offer

- Price: **Rp99.000**
- Digital product.
- Intended for real barber/operator use.
- The product must be represented honestly; no claim of guaranteed revenue.

### Proof threshold

The first proof is achieved only when all are true:

1. A real external customer independently chooses to buy.
2. The customer completes the real production payment.
3. Server-side payment verification succeeds.
4. The customer receives the correct versioned product.
5. The customer acknowledges receipt or otherwise confirms successful delivery.
6. A feedback interaction is obtained.
7. Evidence is recorded without exposing unnecessary personal data.

This is **E4 transaction + E3 external use** at minimum. Customer outcome is still separate.

## 5. Prospect Rule

The first prospect does not need to be a perfect ICP.

The prospect must, however:

- be a real person;
- operate or be meaningfully involved in a barber business;
- understand what is being offered;
- make their own purchase decision;
- not be invented for testing;
- not be coerced into purchasing.

The first goal is learning, not volume.

## 6. What To Learn During The First Conversation

Do not lead with a technical explanation of HOLBERY.

Learn:

1. What operational problem currently costs them time or money?
2. How do they currently manage it?
3. What have they already tried?
4. What makes the problem annoying or expensive?
5. Which part of the BRS package appears immediately useful?
6. What would make Rp99.000 feel worth paying?
7. What would stop them from buying?
8. What would they expect after purchase?
9. What would a successful result look like after using it?

The conversation should produce evidence, not compliments.

## 7. Offer Rule

Use a simple proposition:

> BRS-2026-V1 is a practical starter system for barbers who want a more organized way to manage revenue, customers, pricing, and repeat business.

Then show what is actually included.

Do not claim:

- guaranteed revenue;
- guaranteed customer growth;
- proven market demand;
- proven ROI;
- testimonials that do not exist.

## 8. Transaction Rule

When a prospect wants to buy:

1. Send the canonical HOLBERY product/store URL.
2. Customer uses the production checkout.
3. Customer enters their own details.
4. Customer pays through the live provider.
5. Wait for server verification.
6. Confirm the paid order.
7. Deliver through the protected fulfillment flow.
8. Ask whether the package was received and usable.

No manual database mutation should be used to manufacture a paid state.

## 9. Feedback Rule

Within a reasonable period after delivery, ask:

- Did you receive the package correctly?
- Which part did you use first?
- What was immediately useful?
- What was confusing or unnecessary?
- What problem did it help you handle?
- What is still missing?
- Would you recommend it to another barber? Why or why not?

A negative answer is useful evidence.

## 10. Evidence Capture

Record only the minimum necessary:

- anonymous prospect/customer identifier;
- date;
- acquisition source;
- problem statement;
- objection;
- offer presented;
- purchase decision;
- order reference;
- payment status;
- delivery status;
- feedback;
- observed outcome;
- next product decision.

Do not commit customer PII, payment credentials, private contact details, or private order data to Git.

## 12. Kill / Iterate Rules

### Iterate if

- customer understands the problem;
- customer sees value;
- purchase happens;
- delivery succeeds;
- feedback identifies fixable friction.

### Reposition if

- prospects understand the problem but do not understand the offer;
- objections consistently concern positioning rather than price;
- another use case repeatedly appears stronger.

### Kill or replace if

- repeated qualified prospects do not perceive meaningful value;
- the problem is weak;
- the product does not solve the stated problem;
- willingness to pay remains absent after honest iteration.

No emotional attachment to the first product.
