# 03 — PORTFOLIO ARCHITECTURE

## 1. Portfolio layers

### Layer A — Parent
HOLBERY owns the house strategy.

### Layer B — Child brands
Distinct businesses or propositions with meaningful category or audience differences.

### Layer C — Products
Specific offers sold through one or multiple channels.

### Layer D — Distribution
Own store, marketplaces, social commerce, affiliate, reseller, B2B, and future channels.

## 2. Child-brand categories
A child brand may belong to one of these broad territories:
- Vertical business systems
- Consumer products
- Digital products
- Commerce services
- B2B solutions
- Future specialist categories

These are portfolio buckets, not mandatory public descriptors.

## 3. Child-brand gate
A candidate child brand must answer five questions:
1. What problem does it own?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why does it need a separate identity?
4. What can it become beyond the first offer?
5. Why is HOLBERY the right parent?

## 4. Portfolio economics
Each child should have a measurable economic role:
- revenue generator
- acquisition engine
- recurring-revenue product
- distribution asset
- strategic capability
- audience / demand asset

## 5. Cannibalization rule
Do not create two child brands that compete for the same customer with nearly identical propositions unless there is a deliberate portfolio reason.

## 6. Sunset rule
A child brand may be paused, merged, sold, or retired. HOLBERY owns the portfolio architecture rather than protecting every brand forever.

## 7. Current known separation rules
- Bozq One System remains private/internal and is not a public HOLBERY child brand.
- Tolvey/Tolva remains separate until explicitly assigned.
- Kestora, if deployed as a vertical business-system brand, should enter through the child-brand onboarding gate rather than being assumed as already launched under HOLBERY.
