# 15 — PARENT IP AND LEGAL OWNERSHIP MODEL

## Purpose
A brand cannot function as a true parent merely by having a parent-sounding name. Ownership and control must be reflected in the legal and operational structure.

## 1. Recommended conceptual model

```
Legal Owner / Operating Entity
            │
            └── HOLBERY (Master Parent Brand)
                    │
                    ├── Child Brand IP
                    ├── Shared Platform IP
                    ├── Domains
                    ├── Commerce Infrastructure
                    └── Portfolio Records
```

The exact legal entity structure requires jurisdiction-specific professional advice.

## 2. Separate three things

### A. Legal entity
The organization that owns contracts, assets, accounts, and obligations.

### B. Parent brand
HOLBERY — the strategic identity presented to the market as the house.

### C. Child brands
Individual commercial identities operating under the portfolio.

Do not assume these three must always have identical names.

## 3. Parent-level assets
Where appropriate, parent-level control should cover:
- HOLBERY trademarks
- core domains
- master wordmark
- shared software IP
- shared data schemas
- shared commerce architecture
- portfolio registry
- group-level documentation

## 4. Child-level assets
Depending on structure:
- child trademark
- child domain
- child product assets
- category-specific content
- campaign assets
- customer proposition
- product formulations / specifications

## 5. IP licensing model
If a central legal entity owns a child brand, internal governance should document:
- who owns the IP
- who operates the brand
- who may use the brand
- what happens on sale / shutdown
- where domains and social accounts are controlled
- how shared IP is separated

## 6. Clearance rule
Strategic lock of HOLBERY does not equal legal clearance.

Before commercial launch:
- search exact and confusingly similar marks
- verify relevant Nice classes
- check Indonesia / DJKI
- check international registries as needed
- secure domains
- secure important social identities
- retain evidence of searches

Use qualified IP counsel for final filing and conflict decisions.
