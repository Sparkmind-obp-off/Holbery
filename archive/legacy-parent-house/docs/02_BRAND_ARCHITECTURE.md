# 02 — BRAND ARCHITECTURE

## 1. Core hierarchy
```
HOLBERY
├── Child Brand A
│   ├── Products
│   ├── Services
│   └── Channels
├── Child Brand B
│   ├── Products
│   ├── Services
│   └── Channels
├── Child Brand C
└── Future Brands
```

Shared infrastructure sits beneath the portfolio rather than becoming the identity of every child:

```
HOLBERY COMMERCE
HOLBERY OPERATIONS
HOLBERY DATA
HOLBERY DISTRIBUTION
HOLBERY SYSTEMS
```

## 2. Parent visibility model

### Endorsed
Use when trust from the parent materially helps the child brand.

Example:

**Child Brand**  
*A HOLBERY company*

### House-owned
Use when the child brand is clearly independent in market and the parent mostly operates behind the scenes.

### Parent-facing
Use for corporate, investor, partner, recruitment, legal, and group-level communications.

## 3. Naming hierarchy
Use these consistently:
- **HOLBERY** — master parent brand
- **[Child Brand]** — independent market identity
- **[Product]** — product-level name
- **[Collection / Program]** — campaign or grouped offer

Do not create unnecessary layers.

## 4. What belongs at parent level
- governance
- portfolio
- shared commerce
- group capability
- legal/corporate identity
- strategic partnerships
- parent-level reporting

## 5. What belongs at child-brand level
- customer proposition
- category positioning
- product naming
- audience language
- campaign voice
- category-specific visual identity

## 6. Non-negotiable rule
Child brands may be visually different, but their existence must be legible inside the portfolio. A new child brand must have a reason to be separate.
