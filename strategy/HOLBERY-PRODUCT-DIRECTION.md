# HOLBERY PRODUCT DIRECTION SYSTEM

**Status:** PRIMARY PRODUCT GOVERNANCE  
**Version:** V1.0  
**Date:** 2026-10-06

## 1. Purpose

This document prevents HOLBERY from becoming a sequence of disconnected products created only because they might sell.

Every product must have a reason to exist inside the parent brand.

## 2. Product Thesis

HOLBERY products should create practical value and strengthen one or more of:

- operator capability;
- business capability;
- workflow/system capability;
- commercial capability;
- knowledge or reusable infrastructure.

Products should be capable of becoming evidence for the larger HOLBERY thesis.

## 3. Product Ladder

### Level 0 — Problem Signal
A repeated problem, demand signal, operational pain, or underserved job is identified.

### Level 1 — Product Thesis
Define user, problem, promise, mechanism, scope, and why HOLBERY should own it.

### Level 2 — Prototype
Build the smallest useful artifact.

### Level 3 — Evidence
Verify usefulness through actual use, tests, feedback, delivery, or customer behavior.

### Level 4 — Paid Product
Introduce a legitimate paid offer when the value proposition is sufficiently clear.

### Level 5 — Product-Market Evidence
Look for repeat demand, referrals, repeat purchases, retention, or measurable outcomes.

### Level 6 — System
If the product repeatedly solves the same problem, convert reusable knowledge into a system.

### Level 7 — Venture
If the system develops independent economics, identity, customers, and operating logic, evaluate whether it should become a venture.

## 4. Product Admission Gate

A candidate must have:

- target user;
- problem;
- desired outcome;
- product thesis;
- evidence source;
- owner inside HOLBERY;
- initial scope;
- success metric;
- kill criteria;
- legal/commercial feasibility;
- relationship to parent brand.

No candidate moves directly from "idea" to "listing."

## 5. Product Evidence

Evidence may include:

- verified customer use;
- customer feedback;
- successful delivery;
- completed workflow;
- measured outcome;
- paid transaction;
- repeat purchase;
- referral;
- support resolution;
- independent validation.

Evidence must be labeled honestly.

Engineering tests prove engineering behavior. They do not prove customer demand.

## 6. Product Health

Each live product is reviewed across:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Direction | Does it still fit HOLBERY? |
| Value | Does it solve a real problem? |
| Evidence | What proves that? |
| Demand | Are people actively seeking it? |
| Conversion | Do qualified prospects buy? |
| Delivery | Can HOLBERY fulfill reliably? |
| Economics | Is contribution positive or strategically justified? |
| Learning | Is it teaching us something useful? |
| Brand | Does it strengthen or weaken trust? |

## 7. Kill Rules

Kill, pause, or redesign when:

- the problem is not real;
- the user is undefined;
- evidence repeatedly contradicts the thesis;
- delivery is unreliable;
- economics cannot work;
- the product damages trust;
- the product has no strategic relationship to HOLBERY;
- the team is maintaining it only because it already exists.

Do not kill solely because the first experiment is small.

## 8. First Product Principle

BRS-2026-V1 remains the first concrete product proof asset.

The objective is not to force BRS to become a huge business.

The objective is to learn:

**Can HOLBERY identify a real problem, build a useful product, package it responsibly, sell it, deliver it, and learn from the customer?**

If yes, the product has fulfilled an important strategic role even before scale.

## 9. Product Lifecycle

```
SIGNAL
→ THESIS
→ PROTOTYPE
→ EVIDENCE
→ OFFER
→ CUSTOMER
→ LEARNING
→ ITERATION
→ SYSTEM / SCALE / KILL
```

No stage should be skipped merely to manufacture activity.
